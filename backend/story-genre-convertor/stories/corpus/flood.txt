The reservoir had to take water from somewhere and give it back somewhere, and the somewhere was the whole of Arun Kelkar's job.

Two valleys sat below the new dam. Ghatpuri, on the east bank, had four hundred people, a school, and a road that had been metalled the previous year. Sondhe, west, had ninety people and no road worth the name. The spillway could be cut on either side. Whichever side it was cut, that valley would take the overflow in a bad monsoon — not every year, but in the years it mattered, and everyone understood that the years it mattered were becoming more frequent.

Kelkar spent six weeks on the survey. He was not a distinguished engineer and he knew it; he was a thorough one, which is a different thing and rarer.

What the survey found was that the east side was wrong. Not marginally. The rock under Ghatpuri's ridge was fractured in a way that meant a cut there would widen unpredictably, and a widened cut does not flood a valley in a controlled way, it removes the bottom of it. West was ugly and west was fair. Sondhe would lose eleven hectares of grazing in a bad year, and the water would sit for a week, and it would go.

He was three days from filing when the Ghatpuri delegation came to the site office.

They were not thugs. That was the thing he found hardest to explain afterwards. They were a schoolteacher, a man who ran the cooperative, and a woman who had lost a brother in the last flood, and they had brought a folder of their own — photographs, a petition, the road. They talked for two hours. And at the end the cooperative man stayed behind, and said that there was a fund, and that the fund could be directed, and that a man who had spent six weeks in a tent might reasonably be compensated for the wear on his health.

He named a figure. It was four years of Kelkar's salary.

Kelkar said no. He said it politely, because the man had not threatened him and had, in his own way, been honest about what he was doing. Then he filed the survey, and the spillway was cut west, at Sondhe, exactly as the rock required.

The letter arrived at the Board eleven weeks later. It said that the engineer Kelkar had solicited a payment from the Ghatpuri cooperative and, having been refused, had directed the spillway away from Ghatpuri out of spite. It gave a figure. The figure was correct to the rupee, which was the detail that destroyed him, because only two men had ever heard that number spoken and he was one of them.

The inquiry was not a court. It did not have to prove anything. It had to decide whether it was comfortable, and it was not.

He was not prosecuted. He was moved to a desk in a records office in a city four hundred kilometres away and told that this was not a punishment. His name stayed on the Sondhe survey but the survey was re-examined by a second engineer, and the second engineer confirmed the rock findings in full, and nobody read that report because it confirmed a decision already made by a man now understood to be compromised.

He worked in the records office for nine years. He was thorough there too.

In the ninth year the monsoon came in hard and stayed, and the spillway at Sondhe took water for six days, and eleven hectares of grazing went under and came back. In Ghatpuri the school stayed dry. Somebody at the Board pulled the file because a journalist had asked a question about spillway siting, and found the second engineer's report, and read it, which was more than anyone had done in a decade.

They printed a correction. It was a paragraph. It used the phrase *no evidence of impropriety*, which is not the same as any of the things Kelkar had wanted said, and it appeared on a page that also carried the cricket.

A young engineer wrote to him afterwards, care of the records office, to ask about the fracture patterns. It was a technical letter with no apology in it and no sympathy, and it asked six precise questions about rock he had walked over thirty years before.

He answered it that evening. He wrote nine pages. He had been waiting, it turned out, not to be believed but to be consulted, and those are not the same thing either.
